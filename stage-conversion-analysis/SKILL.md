# Stage conversion analysis

How many deals enter each funnel stage and how many move on — each stage's conversion, the overall win rate from first stage to won, and which stage is losing the most deals.

Family: Measurement · Format: .xlsx · Domain: commercial

## What it is

A deal that does not move out of a funnel stage is a loss the same way a unit failing inspection is: stage conversion is first-pass yield applied to a sales pipeline. Follow one cohort of deals (say, everything qualified in one quarter) through to won or lost, and for each stage count the deals that entered and the deals that moved on. From that the tool works out each stage's conversion, the overall win rate from first stage to won (the stage conversions multiplied together, which is always lower than any one of them because losses compound), and names the weakest stage.

## When to use it

Use it when:

- You can pull real per-stage counts from the CRM — how many deals entered a stage and how many advanced to the next one — for a bounded segment, not the whole blended pipeline.
- You want to know which specific stage is losing the most deals, not just an overall win rate.
- Leads come in but few turn into deals, and you need to find the stage where most of them stop moving.
- You want an overall win rate that reflects the real compounding loss across every stage, not an average of each stage's rate.

Not when:

- You only have an overall win rate with no stage-level breakdown — that's a start, but it can't tell you which stage to fix.
- You want time-based findings — lead time, flow efficiency, which stage is capping throughput — that's the sales funnel value stream map, the funnel flow efficiency calculator, or the bottleneck analysis.
- The pipeline segment blends deal types with genuinely different natural conversion patterns — scope down to one comparable segment first, same discipline as every other VSM-family artifact.

## How to fill it in

- **scope** — Name the deal source or segment and the start and end stage — typically sales-qualified lead to closed-won or closed-lost. Keep to one comparable segment; a blended pipeline produces a conversion number that describes neither.
- **stages** — Pick one cohort, such as the deals that became sales-qualified in one quarter, and wait until they have all been won or lost. List each stage in order with how many of that cohort's deals entered it and how many moved on. Take the counts from the CRM's stage history, not a gut sense that "about half fall through."
- **rolledYield** — Calculated from the stage counts — don't type it. Check each stage's conversion against your counts, that the weakest stage is one the sales team would expect, and that the overall win rate matches deals won ÷ deals in at the first stage. If it does not, the counts are not one cohort.
- **narrative** — Write a few sentences on why the weakest stage loses deals, from what reps or RevOps have actually seen — call notes, lost-deal reasons, the deals themselves. If nobody has checked a reason yet, say it's still a guess.
- **countermeasures** — List what you'll do about the weakest stage, ranked by impact and effort, each with an owner and a date. Aim every countermeasure at the stage the data names as weakest, not the one that feels most urgent.

## What good looks like

The example below follows the 200 outbound mid-market deals qualified in October to December 2025 through five stages to won or lost. Demo to proposal is the weakest stage: 70 of 110 deals moved on (64%), down from 70% for the cohort before, the largest single-stage loss in the funnel. Overall, 40 of the 200 were won (20%), down from 22–23% for the two earlier cohorts. Reps report prospects asking for pricing before the demo, but nobody has counted those deals yet, so the first countermeasure tags them before any fix is tried.

## Common mistakes

- **Using period counts instead of following a cohort** — Deals that entered a stage this quarter are not the same deals that moved on this quarter: some came from last quarter's pipeline, some will close next quarter. Dividing one quarter's counts gives conversions that can top 100% or swing with timing alone. Follow one cohort from first stage to won or lost.
- **Estimating stage counts from memory instead of pulling the CRM's stage history** — Reps remember the memorable deals, not the typical ones. The stage-transition counts in the CRM are the ground truth.
- **Treating every stage below 100% conversion as equally worth fixing** — Name the one weakest stage with conviction and work on that; a list of every stage with some loss spreads effort until nothing moves.
