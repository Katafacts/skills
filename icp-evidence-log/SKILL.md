# Ideal customer profile (ICP) evidence log

Turn win and loss notes and discovery excerpts into the few patterns that should shape your ideal customer profile (ICP): each observation logged, tagged with a theme and counted, the way a voice of the customer (VOC) evidence log works, so the profile rests on evidence rather than opinion.

Family: Voice of the customer · Format: .xlsx · Domain: commercial

## What it is

An ICP evidence log is the same clustering discipline as the VOC evidence log — a theme drafted per entry, freely reassignable, with the tally recomputing live — pointed at win/loss call notes, discovery-call excerpts, and competitor mentions instead of product-feedback quotes. It matters because an ideal customer profile written from assumption describes the customer you hope for; one built from what happened in real won and lost deals describes the customer you actually win.

## When to use it

Use it when:

- You have real win/loss call notes or discovery-call excerpts and want to test whether the ICP canvas's assumptions actually hold up against what happened in real deals.
- You suspect a pattern in why deals stall or close fast (a trigger, a buying-committee shape, a segment signal) but haven't clustered the evidence to see if it's real or anecdotal.
- You want the vital-few fit signals named with a defensible tally, not just a sales leader's gut sense of 'our best deals all look like X.'

Not when:

- You don't have real call notes or deal records to draw from yet — this clusters evidence that already exists, it doesn't generate deal history from nothing.
- You only have two or three deals total — not enough material for a real pattern to emerge from coincidence.
- You already know the answer and just want it confirmed — genuine synthesis means letting the entries suggest the clusters, not the other way around.

## How to fill it in

- **scope** — Say which deals or accounts this evidence covers and what prompted collecting it — sharpening the ICP canvas, testing anti-ICP disqualifiers, or something else. Then give the total behind the sample: how many deals were decided in the period, how many were won, and how many have notes in this log. Five wins and five losses can't carry '3 of 3' until you know how many deals there were and how many were never written up.
- **entries** — Enter each win or loss note, discovery excerpt or competitor mention you've gathered, with its source, source type and date. Copy the actual words where you can; a summary loses the detail that makes a pattern visible. Mark each entry won or lost by how its deal ended, so every pattern gets a win rate, and log each deal only once per pattern so the win rate counts deals, not repeated quotes.
- **themeSummary** — Give each entry a theme, then read the themes across all the entries; the tally updates as you reassign them. Name each theme in plain words taken from the evidence, and treat a theme with only one or two entries as a hint, not a pattern.
- **narrative** — Write a few sentences on what the top themes suggest about who your ideal customer is — and who it isn't. Use only what the entries show.
- **nextSteps** — List what the team will do with this — update the ICP canvas, add a disqualifier, change a qualification step — ranked by impact and effort, each with an owner and a date.

## What good looks like

The example below logs 14 entries from Beacon Analytics' first quarter of 2026, covering 9 of the 11 deals decided by 10 March (won or lost; 5 won) and a trial group. Five patterns: a buying trigger (outgrowing spreadsheet workarounds, 3 of 4 entries won), an exec sponsor engaged early in 3 of the 5 wins and its opposite, stalled on a single champion, in 3 of the 4 noted losses, a company-size floor (teams under 20 staff never converted), and an unplanned security review that is a gap in the seller's own process rather than a customer trait. Because the exec-sponsor pattern shows only in wins, it may be a result of a healthy deal, so it is tested on new deals before it shapes the profile.

## Common mistakes

- **Clustering win notes only, leaving losses out of the evidence set.** — A pattern that only explains why deals win says nothing about why similar-looking deals lose — the anti-ICP disqualifiers this evidence should feed depend on the loss entries just as much as the wins.
- **Treating a single memorable deal as a pattern.** — One vivid story isn't a cluster; it needs enough independent entries to mean something beyond one account's specifics.
- **Pulling quotes from memory instead of the actual call notes or customer relationship management (CRM) system's history.** — A pattern built from what a sales leader remembers inherits every bias in that memory. Work from real notes, not recall.
- **Building a pattern seen only in wins straight into the profile.** — It may be a result, not a cause: executives join deals that are already going well. Test it as a leading check first. Log it on every new deal from the start and compare win rates with and without it a quarter later.

## What it connects to

Upstream:

- **Win-loss interview guide** (`win-loss-interview-guide`) — Interviews with buyers after a deal closes, won or lost, are the richest entries this log gets. Run them with the guide so every deal is asked the same questions.
- **Discovery call standard work** (`discovery-standard-work`) — Discovery calls run the same way every time give this log comparable notes, won and lost, from the start of every deal rather than only at the end.

Downstream:

- **Ideal customer profile (ICP) canvas** (`icp-canvas`) — A theme with real weight behind it — a trigger, a buying-committee pattern — is exactly what should update the ICP canvas's definition, not sit unused in a log.
- **Anti-ideal customer profile (anti-ICP)** (`anti-icp`) — A loss-side theme (a size floor, a process gap) is a disqualifier candidate — the anti-ICP list should trace back to evidence like this, not a sales leader's unstated assumptions.
- **Deal A3 — win-rate root cause analysis** (`deal-a3`) — A theme that concentrates in lost pipeline value, not just deal count, is exactly the kind of finding a deal A3's Pareto would chase to root cause.

## Where AI helps

Judgement — stays with you:

- Deciding whether a drafted cluster genuinely reflects a real deal pattern, or moving an entry to a different theme
- Deciding which theme is real fit signal versus coincidence worth watching, not yet acting on

Analysis — AI helps:

- Drafting a first-pass theme label per entry from its actual content
- Drafting the narrative from the top themes
- Flagging when a theme is thin (one or two entries) rather than a defensible pattern

Drudgery — automated:

- Computing theme counts and percentages, recomputed live on every reassignment
- Identifying the vital-few top themes
- Exporting to xlsx in the house format
