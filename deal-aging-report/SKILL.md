# Deal aging report

Which open deals have sat in their current stage too long: each deal's days in its stage read against an age limit for that stage, with its value, rep and next step, so you chase the vital few before they go cold.

Family: Flow and pull · Format: .xlsx · Domain: commercial

## What it is

A deal that sits in one pipeline stage for weeks is going cold: the buyer's attention drifts, and the forecast still counts it as if it were moving. This report lists every open deal with one number, the days it has spent in its current stage, next to its value, its rep and its next step. Each stage gets an age limit: the most days a deal should sit there, usually set from how long most of last quarter's won deals took (the 85th percentile: 85 in 100 won deals were through in that time or less). A deal older than its stage's limit is flagged over limit. A deal exactly on its limit today is marked at limit, because tomorrow it is over unless something moves. The totals show how much pipeline value is over and at its limit, and the stage counts show whether you have one stuck deal or a stage where deals pile up, which need different fixes.

## When to use it

Use it when:

- You can get the date each open deal entered its current stage from the customer relationship management (CRM) system's stage history.
- You want to know which deals need a call this week, and how much pipeline value is stuck, not just that the pipeline feels slow.
- You have last quarter's won deals to set the stage limits from.
- You run a weekly pipeline review or pod huddle and want it to start from the deals that are stuck rather than the deals that are loudest.

Not when:

- You want to know how long finished deals took from start to close, or where they waited: that is a sales funnel map.
- You want to know which stage loses the most deals: that is a stage conversion analysis.
- You only have a pipeline total, with no stage-entry date per deal. There is nothing to measure an age from.
- The pipeline mixes deal types whose stages take very different times (self-serve and enterprise). Run one report per comparable segment.

## How to fill it in

- **scope** — Say which pipeline the report covers (a team, a pod or a segment) and the date you are measuring on. Keep to one comparable segment: blending small and large deals gives limits that fit neither. Note what the values mean (annual subscription value, for example) so nobody adds them to something else.
- **items** — List every open deal with its rep, its value, its current stage, the date it entered that stage and its next step with a date. Ages count calendar days, weekends included. Take the stage-entry date from the CRM's stage history, not from memory and not the date the deal was created: a deal that moved stage yesterday is not stuck, however long discovery took. Write the next step as it really stands; "None booked" is an answer, and a useful one.
- **stages** — List your stages in pipeline order and give each an age limit in days. Take last quarter's won deals, list how many days each spent in the stage, sort them, and read off the value 85% of the way up the list; round to whole days. Set one limit per stage, never one for the whole pipeline: a quick discovery and a long negotiation need different lines. Revisit them each quarter. Each deal's age, its status and each stage's counts, average age and value over the limit are calculated. A stage with no limit flags nothing and says so.
- **summary** — Calculated: how many deals are over their stage's limit and how many are at it today, what they are worth against the whole open pipeline, and the oldest open deal. Check the oldest deal is really still alive before you spend time on it: a deal nobody will take on is better closed as lost than counted in the forecast.
- **narrative** — Ask the reps why each deal over its limit is stuck, then write a few sentences. Say whether it is one deal's own problem (a champion who left) or a stage where deals pile up (three of five over the same limit), because those need different fixes. Name the deals at their limit today.
- **countermeasures** — Two or three actions at most, each with one owner and a date, each tied to a named deal or to the stage where deals pile up, so next week's report shows whether it worked. When deals pile up in one stage, the first action is to stop starting: hold new deals out of that stage (or the one before it) until it is back under its limit. Give every deal at its limit a dated next step the same day.

## What good looks like

The example below is Beacon Analytics' mid-market pod on Monday 16 March 2026: 14 open deals worth $331,200 a year across discovery, demo, proposal and negotiation, with limits of 14, 10, 14 and 21 days set from last quarter's won deals. Four deals are over their limit, worth $108,000. Three of them are in demo (Union Freightways at 21 days, Corbel Health at 17 and Stanmore Fleet Co at 12, against 10), so demo is a stage where deals pile up while proposals wait behind new demos. The first countermeasure stops starting: no new demos are booked until each demo deal at or over its limit has a proposal date this week. The fourth is Fenwick & Co, 55 days in proposal against 14 and the oldest open deal, stuck because its champion is on leave; the other proposal deals are 6 and 11 days old, so the fix is a second contact there, not a change to the stage. Three deals worth $96,000 are at their limit today: Halden Foods (14 days in discovery, with no next step booked), Tillman Retail (10 days in demo) and Brightwater Utilities (21 days in negotiation). Northline Freight is within its limit at 14 of 21 days in negotiation.

## Common mistakes

- **One age limit for the whole pipeline.** — A slow stage such as negotiation always looks stuck against a pipeline-wide line, and a fast one never does. Each stage needs its own limit.
- **Measuring age from when the deal was created.** — A deal that moved stage yesterday after a long discovery looks stuck when it is not. Age in the current stage tells you where it is stuck.
- **Flagging deals against their stage's average age.** — The average includes the stuck deals themselves, so it rises as they age and a stage full of slow deals never flags any of them. A fixed limit from won deals does not move with the deals it is measuring.
- **Chasing every flagged deal the same way.** — One stuck deal needs a fix for that deal. Several deals over the same stage's limit point to how the stage runs, and calling each buyer will not fix it.
- **Chasing stuck deals while still booking new ones into the same stage.** — Deals open in a stage are roughly deals moving through it per week times weeks in the stage. If reps keep booking demos while proposals wait, the demo stage stays as full and every new deal ages there too. Stop starting: hold new demo bookings until the proposals are out, then book again.
- **Keeping dead deals open.** — A deal nobody at the buyer will take on inflates the pipeline and the forecast and hides the deals that can still be saved. Set a date to revive it or close it as lost.

## What it connects to

Upstream:

- **Stage conversion analysis** (`stage-conversion-analysis`) — Shows which stage loses the most deals. A stage that loses many deals and also has many over its age limit is the first place to look.

Downstream:

- **Sales funnel value stream map** (`sales-funnel-map`) — When deals keep piling up in one stage, map the whole funnel: it shows where finished deals waited and which stage holds the work back. This report's stuck deals are its work-in-progress counts.
- **Deal A3 — win-rate root cause analysis** (`deal-a3`) — When the same stage keeps filling with deals over its limit week after week, a deal A3 works out why.
- **Mutual action plan** (`mutual-action-plan`) — A late-stage deal near its limit gets a dated plan agreed with the buyer, so both sides can see what moves it forward and who owns each step.

## Where AI helps

Judgement — stays with you:

- Setting each stage's age limit, and deciding when a limit should change
- Deciding whether a stuck deal is still alive, and when to close it as lost
- Choosing which countermeasure to act on first

Analysis — AI helps:

- Working out each stage's 85th-percentile limit from last quarter's won deals
- Spotting whether flagged deals cluster in one stage or are spread out
- Drafting the narrative from the reps' notes and the deals over and at their limit

Drudgery — automated:

- Computing every deal's age, status and the value over and at its limit
- Pulling stage-entry dates from the CRM's stage history
- Laying the report out and exporting it as a workbook
