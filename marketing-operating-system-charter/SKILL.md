# Marketing operating system charter

Write down how the marketing team runs: a cadence calendar of its forums (how often, when, purpose, inputs, owner, attendees), the recurring decisions each one makes with who decides and who is consulted, and the few measures each one reviews. Meeting hours and counts are computed, and decisions with no forum or no one deciding, forums that decide nothing or aren't meeting as planned, and measures nobody reviews are flagged.

Family: Daily management · Format: .xlsx · Domain: commercial

## What it is

Every team has an operating system, whether or not anyone wrote it down: the meetings it holds, the decisions it makes again and again, and the numbers it watches. When it isn't written down, the same problems keep showing up. A budget move gets made in a corridor because nobody knows which meeting owns it. A monthly meeting runs for a year without deciding anything. A report gets built every week and nobody looks at it. This charter writes the system down in three short tables. The cadence calendar lists the standing forums: how often, when and for how long each meets, what it is for, what is brought to it, who runs it, who attends, and how many times it actually met in the last quarter. The decision rights list the recurring decisions (approve a campaign, move budget between channels, approve a claim for use outside the company, change what counts as a qualified lead), each with the one forum where it is made, the one role that decides, and who is asked first. The measures list the few numbers the team runs on, each with an owner, a target and the forum that reviews it. The three tables are tied together by forum name, so the gaps show themselves: a decision with no home, a forum with no decision, a number nobody reviews, a meeting on the calendar that doesn't happen.

## When to use it

Use it when:

- Decisions about budget, campaigns or claims get made in different places by different people, and nobody can say where the next one will be made.
- The calendar is full of recurring meetings and people suspect some of them decide nothing.
- A new leader, a reorganisation or a new planning year is a natural moment to write down how the team will run.
- A diagnostic (a metric definition sheet, an attribution check, a review of lost deals) found a decision nobody owns, and it needs a home.

Not when:

- You need to split the work inside one process among roles. Use a responsible, accountable, consulted, informed (RACI) chart for who does what on each task.
- You are planning a one-off improvement event with a start and end date. Use the kaizen event charter.
- You need to decide this year's goals and strategies. That is the revenue plan; this charter is where the plan gets reviewed.
- The team is two or three people who sit together and decide as they go. Writing it down adds little until it grows.

## How to fill it in

- **purpose** — Say which team the charter covers, from when, and where its edges are (for example, marketing through to the hand-off of leads to sales). Write the system as it actually runs today, then change it; a charter of how things are meant to work hides the gaps it exists to find.
- **forums** — List each standing meeting: its name, how often and how long it meets, when (the day, week of the month and time, so the weekly feeds the monthly and the monthly feeds the quarterly), its purpose in one sentence, what is brought to it (the reports, the drafts, the numbers), who runs it and who attends. Name the owner as a role with today's holder. Leave out one-off meetings and one-to-ones; this is the team's shared rhythm.
- **held** — Count from the calendar how many times each forum actually met in the last 13 weeks. The charter compares it with the number planned (13 for weekly, 6 for every two weeks, 3 for monthly) and flags a forum that met fewer than three times in four. Decisions placed in a forum that doesn't meet are decisions waiting.
- **decisions** — List the decisions the team makes again and again, each starting with a verb: approve a campaign within budget, move money between channels, approve a claim for use outside the company, change a lead definition, choose the next message test. Include the ones people argue about. For each, pick the one forum where it is made, the one role that decides, and who is asked first. If nobody knows, leave it blank: that is the finding.
- **measures** — List the few numbers the team runs on, each with the person who reports it and explains a miss, a target, and the forum that reviews it. Use the definitions the team has agreed. More than five in one forum is more than anyone will act on; report the rest instead of reviewing them.
- **checks** — Nothing to enter: the charter works out each forum's hours a year and meetings planned, counts the decisions and measures placed in it, and flags a forum with no owner, no decision, more than five measures or fewer meetings than planned; a decision with no forum or no one deciding; and a measure that no forum reviews or nobody owns.
- **review** — Write what the charter shows and what changes: the decision that gets a home and a decider, the meeting that becomes a written update, the forum that gets a fixed slot. Share it with everyone in it and check it again each quarter against what actually happened.

## What good looks like

The example below is Beacon Analytics' marketing operating system, written by the head of marketing at the end of June 2026 from what actually happened in April to June. Five forums take 74.5 hours a year for someone who attends all of them. Seven of eight recurring decisions have a forum and one role deciding; the eighth, the rule for which channel gets credit for a lead, has neither, and the position-based credit report beside it is the one measure of seven that no forum reviews. The monthly team update decides nothing, and the message and content review met 3 times out of the 6 planned. Two forums, one decision and one measure are flagged, and the review says what changes: the credit rule goes to the quarterly plan and budget review with one person deciding, the update becomes a written note, and the message and content review gets a fixed slot.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing the rhythm as it should be instead of as it is.** — A charter of the intended meetings hides the ones that don't happen and the decisions made elsewhere. Count the meetings from the calendar and place each decision where it was really made last time; then change it.
- **Meetings that only share updates.** — A forum that decides nothing costs every attendee its hours and changes nothing. Give it a decision or turn it into a written note.
- **Two forums, or two people, for the same decision.** — When a decision can be made in two places, each assumes the other has it, or both make it differently. One forum, one role deciding; everyone else is consulted.
- **Every number in every meeting.** — A forum that reviews twenty measures acts on none of them. Keep the few that lead to a decision there and report the rest.
- **Writing it once and filing it.** — Teams drift: meetings get skipped, new decisions appear, people move. Check the charter each quarter against the calendar and what was decided where.

## What it connects to

Upstream:

- **Marketing metric definition sheet** (`marketing-metric-definition-sheet`) — Defines how each measure the forums review is counted, who owns it and its target, so every forum reads the same number.
- **Marketing attribution reality check** (`marketing-attribution-reality-check`) — Shows how much a channel's credit depends on the rule chosen. The charter gives that rule a forum and someone to decide it.
- **Message testing log** (`message-testing-log`) — The tests need a forum where the next ones are chosen and finished ones are read; the charter names it and who decides.
- **Revenue hoshin kanri X-matrix** (`revenue-hoshin-kanri`) — The year's plan sets the goals; the charter's monthly and quarterly forums are where progress against it gets reviewed.

Downstream:

- **RACI (responsible, accountable, consulted, informed) chart** (`raci-chart`) — When one decision or process needs more detail on who does what, a RACI chart splits the work task by task.
- **Tiered huddle board** (`tiered-huddle-board`) — Where problems need to move quickly from one meeting to the next level up, a tiered huddle board sets the escalation path between the forums.
