# Shift handover

Pass the shift on face to face at the board, in the same order every time: safety, status against plan, open problems with what was done and what's next, equipment and materials, people, and the next shift's first hour, signed by both leads. Checked for you: problems with no next action or owner, problems past their time to go up the escalation ladder, problems carried shift after shift, and a handover with no safety.

Family: Daily management · Format: .xlsx · Domain: core

## What it is

A shift handover is the few minutes when one shift passes the work to the next. Done well, the two leads stand at the board together, go through it in the same order every time, and both sign what was said. The order matters. Safety comes first, so a hazard is never squeezed out at the end. Then quality: any suspect or held product, how many, where it is and what happens to it, so held parts are passed on as parts, not as a number. Then how the shift did against its plan, and why. Then every problem still open, each with what has been done, what happens next, who does it and by when. Then the state of the equipment and materials, then people, and last, what the incoming shift must do in its first hour. The handover's real job is to make sure nothing open leaves without an owner, and that a problem's clock doesn't stop because the people changed: if the escalation matrix says a problem goes up a level 45 minutes after it was raised, that still holds across the shift change. The incoming lead's signature means "I've got it". After the board, the two walk the area together, so the incoming lead sees the equipment and the held parts for themselves.

## When to use it

Use it when:

- Work runs across two or more shifts, and things get lost or done twice at the change.
- The next shift keeps finding problems the last one already knew about.
- A problem sat over a shift change long past the point it should have gone up to someone senior.
- You want a record of what was open at the change, and who took it on.

Not when:

- One team does the whole job with no change of people. Plan the day at the daily huddle instead.
- You need to find why a problem keeps coming back. The handover passes it on; a 5-Whys or an A3 finds the cause.
- You want a full shift report for managers. Keep the handover to what the next shift has to act on; the shift board carries the numbers.

## How to fill it in

- **header** — Write the area, the date, the handover time and who is handing over to whom. Overlap the shifts by 15 minutes: 5 to 10 minutes at the board, face to face, then walk the area together so the incoming lead sees the state of the equipment and the held parts for themselves. A note left on the desk can't answer questions.
- **safety** — Start here, every time. Injuries and near misses this shift, then hazards and open safety actions the next shift must know about. If there were none, write "none": a blank box can't tell the next shift whether it was checked.
- **holds** — One row for each lot of suspect or held product: what it is, how many, where it is and how it's marked, what happens to it (the decision, or who decides by when) and one owner. A hold passed on as "14 on hold" gets shipped, counted as good or forgotten; a hold with a count, a tag and an owner gets dealt with.
- **plan** — For the few measures the shift is run on (good parts made, changeovers in their slot, parts on hold), write the plan and the actual, and whether higher or lower is better. Give the reason for any gap in a few words, so the next shift knows whether the cause is still there.
- **problems** — One row per problem still open: what is wrong, what has been done so far, the next action, one owner and by when. For anything on the escalation matrix, add the clock time it was raised and the minutes after which the matrix sends it up a level; the sheet works out when that is. Write who it has gone up to once it has. Count how many earlier handovers it has already been through.
- **equipment** — List the machines, tools and materials the next shift depends on, each OK, low or limited, or down or out. For anything not OK, say what is wrong and what is being done about it.
- **people** — Absences, who is covering and what they can and can't do yet, and anyone new or in training.
- **firstHour** — The things the incoming shift must do before anything else, each with an owner and a time. Three or four at most; if everything is urgent, nothing is.
- **signOff** — Once everything has been talked through, both leads type or write their names and the time. The incoming name means "I've got it". Write anything agreed at the board in the notes.
- **checks** — Nothing to enter here. The sheet flags a handover with no safety; a hold with no count, location, decision or owner; a measure behind plan with no reason; an open problem with no next action, no owner, carried over two or more shifts, or past its time to go up and not gone up; equipment with no state or not OK with nothing written; a first-hour action with no owner; and a sign-off missing a name. Settle each one at the board before you sign.

## Common mistakes

- **Handing over by message or a note left on the desk.** — Questions only get asked face to face. Five to ten minutes at the board with both leads, then a walk of the area, catches what the note left out.
- **Passing held parts on as a number.** — "14 on hold" doesn't say which parts, where they are or what happens to them, and suspect product gets shipped that way. Give each hold a count, a location, a decision and an owner.
- **Leaving safety to the end, or out when nothing happened.** — At the end it gets squeezed out. Say "no injuries, no near misses" in so many words, so the next shift knows it was checked.
- **Passing on a problem with no next action or no owner.** — "Maintenance has it" hands the next shift a worry, not a task. Every open problem leaves with what happens next, one name and a time.
- **Letting the shift change restart the escalation clock.** — The ladder's times run from when the problem was raised. In the example the short truck was due to go up at 14:05; handing it on at 14:15 doesn't buy it another 45 minutes.
- **Carrying the same problem from shift to shift.** — A problem on its third handover needs a decision, not more passing on: take it to the huddle or up the ladder.

## What it connects to

Upstream:

- **Escalation matrix** (`escalation-matrix`) — Gives each problem's ladder and the minutes after which it goes up; the handover carries those times across the shift change.
- **Andon signal definition** (`andon-signal-definition`) — Pulls answered during the shift that aren't closed yet become open problems at the handover.
- **SQDIP (safety, quality, delivery, inventory, productivity) shift board** (`sqdip-board`) — The shift board's numbers are the status against plan; the handover adds the reasons and what's still open.
- **Leader standard work** (`leader-standard-work`) — The handover is a fixed slot in the shift lead's routine, at the same time every day.

Downstream:

- **Daily management huddle** (`daily-huddle`) — A problem carried over more than one shift goes to the next huddle for a decision.
- **Countermeasure log** (`countermeasure-log`) — Once a problem is contained, the fix that stops it coming back goes in the log with an owner and a date.

## Where AI helps

Judgement — stays with you:

- Deciding what the next shift really needs to know, and what can wait
- Deciding when a problem needs to go up rather than be passed on
- Agreeing the next action and who owns it, face to face

Analysis — AI helps:

- Spotting problems with no next action or owner, and ones carried shift after shift
- Working out when each problem was due to go up the escalation ladder
- Reading a run of handovers for the problems that keep coming back

Drudgery — automated:

- Copying open problems and times from one handover to the next
- Counting shifts carried and minutes open
- Laying out the handover and exporting it as a workbook
