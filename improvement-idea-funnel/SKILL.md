# Kaizen idea funnel

Every improvement idea in one view: how fast the person who raised it heard back, which stage it's in, which have sat too long, and which have nobody championing them. Small ideas go straight to Just do it; only the big ones become events.

Family: Kaizen events · Format: .xlsx · Domain: core

## What it is

A kaizen idea funnel is the view above any single event: every improvement idea people have raised, where each one is, and whether it's moving. Its main measure is how many days the person who raised an idea waits to hear back, because people keep raising ideas when they hear back fast and stop when ideas vanish. Most ideas are small: the person who raised them can do them within a week, so they go to Just do it, with no charter or event. Only ideas that need a team and a few focused days get chartered. You name the stages and an age limit for each; the days to hear back, each idea's age, the count per stage, the stale ideas and the ideas with no champion are worked out for you. Champions are never guessed: every idea starts unassigned until you name a real person.

## When to use it

Use it when:

- People are raising improvement ideas and you want each one answered quickly and moving, not lost in a suggestion box.
- You want to catch an idea that has sat in a stage longer than you allow before the person who raised it gives up.
- You want to see which ideas need an event and which someone can just do this week.

Not when:

- You're planning a single event that's already decided: that's the kaizen event charter's job.
- You're following up one event that has already run: check its changes held with the sustainment audit.

## How to fill it in

- **stageOrder** — Name your stages in order, each a real decision point: for example Captured, Screened, Just do it, Chartered, Scheduled, Implemented, Declined. Keep Just do it for small ideas the person who raised them can do within a week.
- **stageAgeLimits** — For each stage an idea should move through, set how many days it may sit there before it counts as stale, for example 14 days in Captured. Leave finished stages such as Implemented and Declined without a limit.
- **responseLimitDays** — Set how many days you promise before everyone who raises an idea hears back, even if the answer is "not yet". Five days is a common promise.
- **ideas** — List each idea with where it came from, the day it was raised, the day its author first heard back (blank while they're still waiting), its stage, the date it entered that stage, the measure it should move and a named champion. Name the champion at screening at the latest. The days to hear back and each idea's age are calculated; don't type them.
- **narrative** — Write a few sentences: who is still waiting to hear back, which ideas are stale or blocked, and where ideas pile up. Use only what the funnel shows.
- **countermeasures** — Write the vital few changes to how ideas move, each tied to a finding above, with one owner and a due date.

## What good looks like

The example below is an outpatient clinic's intake funnel: 8 ideas in 7 stages, as of 1 March. The people who raised them heard back in a median of 3.5 days, but that only counts the six who got an answer. Three people have waited longer than the clinic's 5-day promise: two were answered late (after 14 and 10 days), and one, who raised a phone-hold-time idea from a patient survey, has had no answer in 45 days, 31 days over Captured's 14-day limit. The same-day slot pool is already scheduled with no champion, so the funnel flags it as blocked. Three of eight ideas have no champion. A wristband printer idea shows the Just do it route: answered the day it was raised and handed to the front desk to do within 7 days, without a charter.

## Common mistakes

- **Measuring how many ideas come in, not how fast people hear back.** — A full funnel nobody answers teaches people to stop raising ideas. Days to hear back is the number that keeps them coming; count the ideas still waiting, because a median of answered ideas can't see them.
- **Sending every idea towards an event.** — Most ideas are small. Sending them through a charter and a scheduled event makes them wait weeks for a change one person could make this week; let them just do it.
- **Moving an idea past screening with no champion, or naming a placeholder.** — An idea nobody owns doesn't move. A fake champion hides the same gap; leave it unassigned so the check shows it.

Judgement — stays with you:

Analysis — AI helps:

- Picking which waiting, stale or blocked ideas need attention first, and why
- Drafting the narrative from the funnel actually built

Drudgery — automated:

- Working out days to hear back, each idea's age and each stage's average age
- Flagging ideas over their stage's limit and people who waited too long
- Finding ideas past screening with no champion
