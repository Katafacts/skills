# Implementation plan

Milestones toward a real completion date, each owned by your own team, an outside party, or both — with days remaining, overdue and unowned milestones, and a mutuality check computed, not asserted: a plan where every milestone belongs to your own side isn't actually cross-functional.

Family: Improvement events · Format: .xlsx · Domain: core

## What it is

An implementation plan turns a commitment — a countermeasure, an improvement event charter's rollout, a Hoshin catchball agreement — into a real, checkable sequence of milestones converging on a target completion date. Each milestone names who it depends on: your own team, an outside party, or both jointly. Days remaining, which milestones are already overdue, and which have no named owner yet are all computed from what you enter, not asserted. The one check unique to this tool: if every milestone belongs to your own side, that's flagged directly — a plan with zero cross-functional dependency usually means nobody outside the initiating team has actually committed to anything yet, not that the plan is simpler than most.

## When to use it

Use it when:

- A commitment depends on more than one team or person actually doing something, and you want a real, dated sequence instead of a vague understanding.
- You want to know honestly which milestones are already behind schedule and which have nobody named against them yet.
- You're rolling out something (a countermeasure, a new standard, a system change) where an outside team's action is a real dependency, not just your own team's work.

Not when:

- Everything genuinely depends on one person or one team — a plan with no real outside dependency doesn't need this structure; a simple task list will do.
- You don't have a real target completion date yet — pin that down first, or the days-remaining and overdue math has nothing real to measure against.
- You're still deciding what to do, not how to sequence doing it — that's an A3 or an improvement event charter's job; this tool assumes the commitment is already made.

## How to fill it in

- **milestones** — List the milestones in order, each with who it depends on (you, the customer or both), a named owner and a target date. Agree them with the customer rather than writing them alone; a milestone with no named person on their side is the one that slips.
- **coverage** — Calculated from your milestones and dates — don't type it; check the done, overdue and unowned counts match what you know, and that the work isn't all sitting on one side.
- **risks** — List what could realistically make this plan slip — a busy customer team, a missing approval, data that isn't ready. Write each one specifically enough that someone could act on it.
- **narrative** — Write a few sentences on where the plan stands: what's on track, what's overdue or unowned, and the biggest risk to the date. Use only what the plan shows.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing every milestone as something your own team does.** — A plan where every milestone belongs to your own side isn't actually testing whether anyone else has really committed — that's exactly what the mutuality check exists to catch, not a sign the plan is simpler than most.
- **Leaving a milestone unowned and moving on rather than naming the gap.** — An unowned milestone isn't a detail to fill in later — it's a real risk to the completion date, and this tool's whole point is surfacing that honestly instead of letting a plan look more complete than it is.
- **Writing a risk as vague as 'things could change.'** — A risk that isn't specific enough to actually watch for isn't useful — name the real thing that could slip this plan, the same discipline this catalogue applies to every other finding.

## What it connects to

Upstream:

- **RACI (responsible, accountable, consulted, informed) chart** (`raci-chart`) — Agree who owns each task and decision first; the A on a row is usually the right owner for the action that changes it.
- **A3 problem solving** (`a3`) — A countermeasure an A3 identifies is exactly the kind of commitment that needs a real, dated, cross-functional plan to actually happen.
- **Improvement event charter** (`improvement-event-charter`) — Once an improvement event's plan is set, rolling out what it produced across a wider area is what this tool sequences.
