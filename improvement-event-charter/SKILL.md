# Improvement event charter

The opportunity, the objective, who's on the team, and whether you're actually ready to start — one document, not three you'd have to keep in sync, with a readiness checklist you check off before the event begins.

Family: Improvement events · Format: .xlsx · Domain: core

## What it is

An improvement event charter is the one document a team lead fills out before an event starts: what opportunity or problem it addresses, a specific measurable objective, what's in and out of scope, who's on the team and their RACI role, and a readiness checklist so nobody discovers mid-event that the data was never pulled. State the opportunity, objective, scope, and team roughly — a real charter gets drafted, including a checklist tailored to what you've actually described, and a check that exactly one person is Accountable.

## When to use it

Use it when:

- You're about to run a focused improvement event (a few days to a week) and want one document the whole team can point to.
- You want to know honestly whether you're ready to start — which readiness items are actually done and which aren't.
- You want the RACI assignment checked, not just recorded — exactly one Accountable owner, not zero or several.

Not when:

- You're still exploring whether there's a real opportunity here at all — that's a problem statement or a go-see walk, not a charter yet.
- The event is genuinely informal and doesn't need a roster or a readiness gate — a charter for a five-minute fix is overhead, not help.
- The objective would just restate the opportunity with no real number — sharpen it first, or the charter will be as vague as the input.

## How to fill it in

- **opportunityStatement** — Write the problem or opportunity this event addresses, with a number that shows how big it is today. Say where you saw it and why it matters now.
- **objective** — Write the specific, measurable target for the event — a number, a unit and a date. If you don't have a real number yet, say plainly that the goal is still vague rather than inventing one.
- **scopeIn** — List what's explicitly in scope: the process, where it starts and ends, and the area, shifts or teams involved.
- **scopeOut** — List what's explicitly out of scope, so the team doesn't drift into it during the event. Name the tempting nearby work — a related process, a system change — that you're deliberately leaving alone.
- **roster** — List who's on the team and each person's RACI role: responsible, accountable, consulted or informed. Exactly one person should be Accountable — who that is, is your call.
- **readiness** — List the concrete, checkable things that must be ready before the event starts — data pulled, people freed from their usual work, the area booked, the sponsor's time set aside. Tick each one only when it's actually done.
- **narrative** — Write a few sentences on whether the event is ready to run: who's on the team, what's still open on the checklist, and the main risk to the objective.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing an objective that just restates the opportunity with no real number.** — "Reduce onboarding friction" isn't a target — a charter without a measurable objective can't tell the team whether the event actually worked.
- **Assigning more than one person as Accountable, or nobody at all.** — RACI's whole point is a single accountable owner — two blurs responsibility when something goes wrong, and zero means nobody actually owns the outcome.
- **Marking readiness items ready before they actually are, to make the checklist look complete.** — The checklist exists to catch a real gap before the event starts, not after — an honestly incomplete checklist is more useful than a dishonestly complete one.
